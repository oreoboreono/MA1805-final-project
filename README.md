

Description:
The name of my project is a game called ‘The wizard’. In this game, you play the wizard fighting off a horde of monsters with your magic and killing them. You can cast magic by drawing specific shapes to your mouse - which correspond to a specific spell. Each spell can kill the monster of its corresponding type. In the game you have to survive 90 seconds by killing the monsters and not letting the monsters reach the wizard. If you survive, you win and if you fail you lose. All drawings, animation and music is made by me.

Functionality / Mechanics:
Spellcasting: The main game mechanic my game revolves around is the ability to cast spells, where you have drawn out the shape that corresponds to a certain spell. To draw a shape, you click down and place a point. Then you have to drag to another position to place down another point in which there is a vector line that is made in between the two points. Continue placing down points to form a unique shape/rune that you can cast by pressing enter, which deletes all drawing forms. When you cast your spell, the angles formed from the vector lines are compared with the angles of the spells in the game. If you have a match you successfully cast a spell. If you do not have matching angles it does not cast the spell. The main spells in the game are:

 Fireball:  triangle shape - 3 points
 Ice strike: icicle shape - 3 points
 Synaptic charge: eye shape - 6 points
 Earthen blow - square shape - 4 points 
 Holy lance: spear shape - 5 points

These spells take down monsters related to them such as fireball killing the fire monster.

Story:
The main theme behind my game is surveillance. My main character of the game, the wizard, has to fight off monsters based on the issues of surveillance and it is presented that surveillance is the evil force that the wizard has to drive back.  The motifs of eyes are constantly represented throughout my monsters and my game to explore the feeling of being constantly looked at. It also adds to the disturbing atmosphere throughout the game. 

Monsters: The monsters are supposed to represent the problems and explore the topic of surveillance. For the fire monster, the illustration of it is supposed to represent the internet being monitored and blocked through a literal firewall and an eye watching you. It also represents a country that does internet surveillance frequently through the stars in the illustration, that being China. For my second monster, the illustration shows monitoring through security cameras where you are constantly being filmed. The ice written on the camera represents the spell and a major issue that relates to the idea of surveillance, that behind ICE in the USA. My third monster is the earth monster which relates to text messages being surveilled through an illustration of a phone monster and the flag of the country that monitors your messages.  My fourth monster is the idea of AI through a brain which is being used as a tool of surveillance by companies and governments where your private information is constantly being accessed. My final monster is the idea of mass religion as a tool of surveillance and how many religions are weaponised to attack the privacy of individuals. This is displayed through an illustration of an angel doll.

Inspiration:
My inspiration for the art aesthetic of the game is old retro PCs who had displayed in one colour that being green. All my illustrations are green and give off an unsettling atmosphere.
Another inspiration of my game is horror specifically analogue horror which is supposed to show the disturbing matter of the problems of surveillance. Analogue horror has low fidelity graphics and creepy imagery that inspired the direction of my game. One series that particularly inspired the atmosphere is Mandela Catalogue, an analogue horror series.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mandela_Catalogue

Another inspiration for my mechanics and the story is a mobile game called Magic for Touch: Wizard for hire where it is a game about drawing shapes to cast spells to pop balloons that carry intruders and trying to stop them from reaching the floor. This inspired the main subject of the wizard as well as the mechanic I used but instead of casting spells by physically drawing them, I took another approach where I drew them with vectors. 
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nitrome.magictouch&hl=en&pli=1
   
Issues I faced along the way:


One issue was the music creation where I had to learn how to create music and sound effects for my game without any musical background. However I was introduced to a website called BeepBox that allowed me to make my music very easily through giving instruments that I could alter and change and score into a pattern to loop to create music. I played around with this for a while to add on to my game and give a more immersive experience. 
https://www.beepbox.co
Another issue was the amount of time it took to create my original graphics. The issue of drawing and animating for the first time for my game took a while where I needed to research how to export the correct sizes and file types in addition. However I eventually did so and combined the drawings with my code later on. I animated through the apps called Procreate and Amberdraws. 

In addition, one last issue was issue with the noise getting crunchy and pixelated when too many monsters spawned and I had to resolve that by getting an updated version of my p5.js.sound.min online which had a fix to the problem.
